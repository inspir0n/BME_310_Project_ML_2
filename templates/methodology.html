Methodology

What was built, what deliberately deviates from Kufel et al. (2023), and what turned out to be wrong in the paper along the way.

1.

Exactly what we're doing

WHAT?
An EfficientNet-B1 backbone, fine-tuned end-to-end, with a small custom head making 14 independent yes/no predictions — one per disease.
WHY?
A chest X-ray can show multiple conditions at once. Softmax forces outputs to compete for one probability budget; sigmoid lets each disease be assessed independently.
HOW?
ImageNet-pretrained weights, fine-tuned with discriminative learning rates and a cosine schedule, on a leakage-free patient-wise split, with stronger-than-paper augmentation to control overfitting.
PROOF?
Per-label AUC, AUPRC, sensitivity/specificity on a held-out test set — plus Grad-CAM, to check the model is looking at the disease, not an artifact.
2.

Architecture

An EfficientNet-B1 backbone, pretrained on ImageNet, with its own 1000-way head removed and replaced with:

Global average pool → Dropout → Dense(512) → BatchNorm → ReLU → Dropout → Dense(256) → ReLU → Dense(14)

The backbone is fine-tuned end-to-end rather than frozen, using discriminative learning rates — a gentle rate on the pretrained backbone, a faster one on the newly-initialised head — decayed on a cosine schedule across training.

4.

Errata found in the paper

Checking the paper's own numbers against the raw data surfaced several small errors:

  • Unique patients: reported as 32,717 — the actual NIH figure is 30,805.
  • Baltruschat et al.'s average AUC is listed as 0.727 in the paper's comparison table; their own 14 per-label values actually average to 0.806.
  • Parameter counts: EfficientNet-B1 vs DenseNet-121 given as "6.7M vs 8.4M" — measured values are closer to 6.5M and 8.0M.
  • The conclusion states binary cross-entropy "strengthens underrepresented classes." Plain BCE is imbalance-agnostic; it does not do this.
5.

A genuine reproducibility bug

The default pretrained EfficientNet-B1 checkpoint available through the timm library shipped a handful of BatchNorm layers with denormal running_var values (~1e-42) — numerically almost zero. BatchNorm divides by the square root of that variance, so those layers amplified activations by roughly 1020, cascading into unusable logits. It was caught by a simple gate: an untrained network's initial loss should sit at -ln(0.5) ≈ 0.693; this one returned values in the millions. Switching to a different pretrained tag fixed it.

6.

Honest limitations

  • Rare labels are noisy. Hernia has only 227 positive cases in the entire 112,120-image dataset — around 46 land in the test split. An AUC computed from that few positives has real statistical uncertainty.
  • AUC alone overstates usefulness on imbalanced labels. See the Results page for AUPRC and precision alongside AUC.
  • Grad-CAM surfaced a real anomaly. The heatmap for Effusion — one of the best-calibrated labels — lit up image corners rather than the costophrenic angles where fluid actually pools.
  • Domain shift. Thresholds and performance are calibrated on this specific dataset's scanners and population. Expect a real accuracy drop on X-rays from a different hospital.
